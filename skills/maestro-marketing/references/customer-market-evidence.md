# Customer and market evidence

## Purpose

Ground audience, problem, language, and offer decisions in current evidence.
This guide is volatile and normally opens contextual research.

## Evidence plan

Start with a decision: segment, problem priority, willingness, language,
objection, or channel behavior. Choose evidence that can change it:

- direct interviews or support/sales artifacts for motives and language;
- behavioral analytics for observed actions;
- experiments for causal changes;
- market or category sources for external constraints;
- surveys for bounded distribution questions with transparent sampling.

## Decision checks

1. Separate reported belief, observed behavior, and causal inference.
2. Record sampling source, recruitment bias, timeframe, and missing segments.
3. Preserve verbatim language only as evidence, not as proof of prevalence.
4. Triangulate high-impact claims across different evidence modes when
   practical.
5. Convert findings into supported decision, confidence, limitation, and next
   disconfirming test.

## Stop conditions

Search volume, social posts, or competitor copy alone do not establish customer
need. One enthusiastic interview does not establish prevalence. Stale market
evidence must not silently become a stable guide.
