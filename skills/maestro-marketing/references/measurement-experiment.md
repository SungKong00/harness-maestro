# Measurement and experiment

## Purpose

Connect the marketing decision to observable behavior and learning.

## Decision checks

1. Define the decision and primary outcome before choosing a metric.
2. Specify unit, population, exposure, attribution window, and baseline.
3. Pair the primary metric with guardrails for quality, downstream harm, or
   misleading local optimization.
4. State the hypothesis and mechanism; distinguish exploratory from
   confirmatory analysis.
5. Predefine assignment, sample rationale, stopping rule, exclusions, and
   minimum decision-relevant effect when a controlled experiment is feasible.
6. Record instrumentation ownership and verify event semantics before launch.
7. Plan the action for positive, negative, and inconclusive results.

## Reject

Clicks without downstream relevance, retrospective metric shopping, repeated
peeking without correction, and unverified event names are not decision proof.
When randomization is infeasible, label causal limits explicitly.
