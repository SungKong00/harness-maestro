# Claim substantiation

## Purpose

Prevent attractive copy from outrunning the evidence.

## Claim ledger

For every objective or implied claim record:

- exact wording and likely audience interpretation;
- claim type: measurable performance, comparison, testimonial, endorsement,
  guarantee, environmental/health/financial, or subjective puffery;
- supporting artifact and population, conditions, date, and limitations;
- necessary qualification and placement;
- disposition: supported, narrow, research, remove, or specialist review.

## Decision checks

1. Evaluate the overall net impression, not isolated literal words.
2. Substantiation must exist before publication and match the claim's scope.
3. A comparison identifies the baseline, population, method, and time period.
4. Typicality and testimonial implications need evidence; disclosure does not
   repair a false claim.
5. Material connections and limitations must be clear and close to the claim.
6. A qualification that contradicts the headline is not a qualification.
7. High-risk regulated claims require current authoritative research and
   qualified review.

## Stop conditions

Do not convert an internal estimate, cherry-picked example, or model prediction
into an objective claim. When proof is missing, narrow the language or mark the
claim blocked; do not “soften” it with vague wording while preserving the same
impression.
