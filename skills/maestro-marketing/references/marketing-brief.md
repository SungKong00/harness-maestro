# Marketing brief

## Purpose

Turn a vague request into a falsifiable marketing problem before copy or channel
work.

## Required inputs

- business outcome and conversion action;
- audience segment and situation;
- problem or desired progress;
- offer, price or commitment, and eligibility;
- available proof and known objections;
- channel, lifecycle stage, locale, and constraints.

## Decision checks

1. State one primary audience. Separate buyer, user, approver, and influencer
   when they differ.
2. Name the behavior to change, not only an awareness goal.
3. Distinguish a verified customer problem from an internal hypothesis.
4. Explain why the offer is relevant now and what commitment it asks.
5. Rank one primary message, two supporting points, and one primary action.
6. Identify the proof required for each high-impact message.
7. Record non-goals and excluded segments to prevent generic copy.

## Reject or stop when

Stop for research when the audience problem, market category, or evidence is
material and unknown. Reject briefs whose audience is “everyone,” outcome is
only “make it engaging,” or proof is invented after the message.

## Verification

A reviewer should be able to predict what evidence would disprove the brief and
which metric would show the intended behavior changed.
